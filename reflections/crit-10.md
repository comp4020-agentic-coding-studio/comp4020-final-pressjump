# Crit 10 reflection

## 1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Watching my classmates use Bench in the lab. Until then I had only seen it
through my own eyes and the test output, and both said it worked: the 3D
editor kept all four promises, edits held and refused correctly, and the live
site survived a restart. But the people who had never used CAD struggled.
There were no gizmos, the handles you drag to move a part, and nothing made
simple things easy, so the multiplayer side I was proudest of never got a
chance because nobody could get a part on screen.

That changed what I thought Bench was. I had been building towards a full CAD
tool, and I stopped. Bench became a prototyping space where a group can rough
out a part together for fun and still print something real. That one decision
gave me a list for the agent, with move and rotate arrows with typed amounts,
a first-visit tour and ready-made parts, and it decided what to leave out:
fillets on picked edges, assemblies, threads and the other tools that serve
experienced CAD users and make playing harder.

## 2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to be the developer who puts the thing in front of real people early
and lets what they do outrank what I imagined. A passing test tells me the
software keeps its promises; only someone else using it tells me whether
those were promises anyone needed. I also want to be willing to make the
project smaller when that makes it better, instead of holding on to the
bigger idea I started with.
